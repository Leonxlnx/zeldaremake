# Performance profile of the sealed world — take-0116 (`973a21e`), native Radeon 780M

**fable-6, 2026-09-19.** The brief for fable-cursor's `lod-1` lane. The owner's two asks were
"trees should spawn high quality immediately around me" and "the game should run more
efficiently"; this document measures what the sealed world costs on the owner's own machine, what
moving the trees' near-LOD swap distances out to 18 / 25 m and pre-warming the LOD pools around
the player would cost, where the frame's triangles and draw calls go, and which savings would pay
for it — ranked.

Everything here is a **native** measurement: headless Chrome on the owner's laptop (AMD Ryzen 7 260
/ Radeon 780M, 16 threads, 62 GB, Windows 11) with `ZR_NATIVE_GPU=1` (ANGLE on D3D11), 1280×720,
`quality=high`, the fixed-timestep play mode of `gauntlet/scripts/perftrace.mjs`. The numbers are
comparable with Astra's and owner-fable's native runs and **not** with the monitor's SwiftShader
takes. The laptop is shared with astra-local and owner-fable, who capture on it too: every table
says what else was running (`gauntlet/perf/r48/load.log`); the counters (draw calls, triangles,
pool bytes, builds, evictions) do not depend on the load, the milliseconds do.

Evidence: `gauntlet/perf/r48/` (every trace JSON with per-frame rows, the ablation matrix, the
variant provenance, `load.log`).

## 2. The near-LOD machinery as shipped in `973a21e` (read-only facts)

The trees system (`src/world/trees/index.ts`) keeps two "near" LOD layers that only exist around
the live camera:

| layer | what it is | swap in / out | pool cap | pre-fetch radius | shown at once |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| near base (`giant.ts` NEAR_BASE_*) | the lower 5 m of every giant / seated column as a relief bole with buttress fins and foot plants; the plain sweep's lower rings collapse meanwhile | **10 / 13 m** (per-bole overrides 5–18 m in NEAR_BASE_RADIUS_OVERRIDE, chosen so no fixed camera stands inside a band) | 12 MB (≈ 9 of 22 bases) | 22 m | NEAR_BOLE_SLOTS = 6 |
| near canopy (`nearCanopy.ts` NEAR_CANOPY_*) | every eligible lower lobe (centre ≤ 21 m up) of a giant / column rebuilt as twiglets with layered laminae; the far cards / laminae of that lobe fold away in the colour pass only (shadows unchanged) | **22 / 26 m** (3-D to the lobe centre; a lobe a hero camera frames swaps at that camera's distance − 1.5 m, never under 7 m) | 64 MB (≈ 140 of 364 parts) | 34 m | NEAR_CANOPY_SLOTS = 40 lobes + 12 limb dressings |

Both layers live in an LRU geometry pool (`lodPool.ts`, round 42): a part inside its pre-fetch
radius is rebuilt ahead of its swap distance in chunks under a **3 ms per-frame budget**
(NEAR_LOD_BUILD_BUDGET_MS), evicted past the byte cap (least recently used first, then the farthest
wanted), and a part that must be drawn before its build is ready is finished synchronously in the
frame (`syncBuilds` in the report). The comment on the cap says 64 MB "pre-fetches to about the
26 m out-radius"; the report below says what that means on the walk.

Two design consequences matter for the brief:

1. **The fixed frames are protected by distance, not by a flag.** Every swap radius was chosen so
   that no hero camera stands inside a band of a bole / lobe it frames (giant.ts lists the
   distances: A's stair-bank giant 10.2 m off-frame, D's north-west-near 11.6 m at its edge, C's
   9.8 m behind it). Moving the default in-radius to 18 or 25 m puts several hero cameras inside
   the bands — the six-view captures per variant below measure exactly that.
2. **"Immediately high quality around me" is two different costs**: the swap distance (how far
   away a tree is already detailed — triangles per frame) and the pre-fetch / pool (whether the
   detail is *built* before the walker gets there — hitches). The variants separate them.

## 3. Baseline — the sealed world on the walk

`trace-0116-baseline.json` — 2400 fixed steps (40 s) along the plaza → stairs → house → north-path
walk, 1280×720, `--finish`. Box: no sibling captures, but the CPU at 99–100 % from the owner's other
apps throughout (Codex desktop, the Claude app's Chrome, Blender open), which is why the world
built in 254 s here against Astra's 52 s in round 37 — read the medians as an upper bound and the
*split* of the frame as the finding.

| | median | p95 | p99 | max |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **step (JS + GPU completion)** | **142.4 ms** | 242.6 | 360.2 | 5834 (frame 0: 11 programs compiled) |
| world.update (JS) | 16.6 | — | — | 293 |
| render issue (JS) | 22.5 | — | — | 4771 (frame 0) |
| **GPU finish (waiting for the frame)** | **99.8** | 267 | 298 | 425 |
| draw calls | 422 | 475 | 480 | 480 |
| triangles | 9.16 M | 11.1 M | 11.3 M | 11.42 M |

**What the split says.** About 100 of the 142 ms are the GPU finishing the frame, and that number
does not move with the geometry: standing still at the spawn (8.1 M triangles, 421 draws) the
finish is 98 ms; climbing the stairs (10.7 M, 450 draws) it is 100 ms; the correlation between a
frame's triangle count and its GPU wait over the whole walk is **0.04** (0.6 ms per extra million
triangles). The 780M is not vertex-bound on this world; it is **per-pixel bound** — the 4096² sun
shadow map with 12 PCSS taps on every lit fragment, SSAO at half resolution, the god-ray march,
bloom, the softening pass and the tone/grade composite at 1280×720 (the round-38 SwiftShader
matrix in `gauntlet/perf/ABLATIONS.md` pointed the same way: `scale=0.5` −33 %, `fx=off` −28 %,
`shadow=0` −15 %). §5 and §6 measure those knobs natively. The consequence for `lod-1`: **wider
near-LOD swap distances cost the GPU almost nothing on this machine; what they cost is on the CPU
side, in the pool** — which is where the walk's hitches already come from.

**Play mode is over the hero budget.** The fixed hero views draw 8.8 M triangles (W38's ceiling is
9 M); the follow camera at 1.75 m behind Link sees 9.2 M at the median and 11.4 M on the stairs,
because the vegetation's near LOD and the grass tiles ring the camera and the stairs look down over
the plaza's full carpet. Nothing in the rubric measures this; it is the frame the owner plays.

**The hitches.** 63 frames exceed 2× the median (285 ms). Their causes (`spikeCauses`):

| cause | frames | what it is |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `mixed` | 41 | update + issue + finish all elevated — the box's load (other processes) landing on a frame |
| `update:vegetation` | 7 | vegetation re-buckets (≥ 0.6 m of camera travel): **211 ms** at frame 87, 153 ms at 1719, 105 ms at 1839; 386 frames of the 2400 spend > 20 ms in vegetation.update (15.7 s of the 40 s walk) |
| `render-issue` | 6 | three's CPU side of the draw — 3.4 s at frame 276 (`stairs up`) with one program compiled |
| `gpu-upload` | 4 | first uploads: 200 geometries over the walk, most of them the trees' pool builds and grass tiles coming into range |
| `update:trees` | 3 | the near-LOD builds: 141 frames spend > 20 ms in trees.update (5.9 s of the walk), max **233 ms** in one frame |
| `shader-compile` | 2 | 12 programs compiled after the first frame (the depth/colour variants a pose meets first) |

**The LOD pools on the walk** (`poolSeries`, one report every 30 frames):

| | near canopy | near base |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| cap | 64 MB | 12 MB |
| live bytes, whole walk | 63.5–64.0 MB (pinned at the cap from frame 29) | 11.3–11.8 MB (at the cap) |
| parts / resident at the end | 369 / 129 | 22 / 9 |
| builds / evictions in 40 s | **264 / 504** | 19 / 32 |
| synchronous builds | 1 | 0 |
| build ms p50 / p95 / max | 26.9 / 117.1 / 243.3 | — |
| longest single chunk between yields | **231.7 ms** (budget 3 ms) | 0.3 ms |
| demand inside the pre-fetch radius | 125.5 MB at 34 m (48 MB at 22 m, 75 MB at 26 m) | 11.3 MB at 22 m |
| pinned (drawn) bytes | 19–23 MB | 0 |

Two things are wrong with the shipped tuning, and they are what "trees spawn high quality
immediately around me" runs into:

1. **The canopy pool is a third of its demand.** The parts within the 34 m pre-fetch radius weigh
   125 MB on this walk; the cap is 64 MB, so the pool evicts what it just built to make room for
   the next approaching part — 504 evictions and 264 builds in 40 s, 6.6 builds a second at 27 ms
   each in the median. That is 7 s of the 40 s walk spent rebuilding parts the walker had already
   passed, and the "drawn" set is only 19–23 MB. The comment in `index.ts` (64 MB "pre-fetches to
   about the 26 m out-radius") was true for the round-42 world; the round-46 canopy is bigger.
2. **The 3 ms build budget is not honoured.** The chunked build yields between its steps, but a
   single step ran 232 ms; the p95 of a whole build is 117 ms and the max 243 ms — a build that is
   meant to spread over 80 frames lands in one or two. These are the `update:trees` spikes and part
   of the `mixed` ones. The generator's yield points are too coarse for the round-46 near-canopy
   parts (twiglets + up to 2400 laminae each).

**The same walk an hour later, with the box at 30 % CPU** (`trace-0116-baseline-repeat.json`,
23:14 UTC, no other capture running): step **97.1 ms** median / 174.5 p95 / 205.7 p99, GPU finish
78.3, world.update 9.9, render issue 9.6, `ready` 160 s, 41 spikes — the same draw calls,
triangles and pool behaviour (320 builds / 558 evictions / 1 synchronous, longest chunk 28.9 ms
against the 3 ms budget, p50 build 10.5 ms). So about 45 ms of the first run's 142 ms were the
other apps' load, and the frame's floor on this GPU at 1280×720 is **~78 ms of pixel work**
whatever the geometry does; the pool's churn is a property of the world, not of the load, though
its cost per build halves when the CPU is free. The variant comparisons in §5 use the counters and
the update columns for that reason; the clean lod18 / lod25 repeats in §5.4 ran at the same low
load as this repeat and are compared with it.

**Load time.** `ready` 254 s natively under this load (world build 134 s: terrain 37 s, structures
33 s, vegetation 25 s, trees 13.5 s, rocks 11 s). The `--warmup` run (`?warmup=1`, what the
walkable build does before its first frame) built in 359 s and spent **106 s in the warm-up**
(compile 17.9 s, textures 4.9 s, the warm pass — every mesh visible, the shadow window widened to
the world — **83 s**); it then lost its page ("Target closed") on the first traced frame — at the
minute another headless Chrome (the site's QA screenshots) was launched beside it, which is the
failure mode found later in the matrix: **two puppeteer launches within seconds of each other on
this laptop detach one of the pages** ("frame got detached" / "Navigating frame was detached"
before the world loads). Every capture in this document after that discovery ran alone; the
warm-up retry is in §5. The 106 s warm-up on an integrated GPU is a number for the walkable build
regardless: on this box it is 40 % of the time from click to first frame.

The retry, alone on a quiet box (`trace-0116-warmup.json`, 23:21 UTC): world build 66 s, then
**warm-up 77.8 s** — compile 11.5 s (141 programs, parallel), textures 2.1 s (52), **the warm pass
64.2 s** (867 meshes exposed, 875 geometries uploaded) — `ready` at 145 s against the plain run's
160 s (which paid its uploads later). What it buys: the first frame **0.41 s instead of 2.9 s**
(0 programs compiled at the first frame instead of 11), **no shader compile anywhere on the
walk** (0 against 12 first-use compiles, each a 100–400 ms frame), geometry uploads 24 against
96 over the first ten seconds; the steady frame is identical (idle 90.7 vs 90.8 ms). What it
costs: 78 s of black screen before the first frame on an integrated GPU, 82 % of it the one
render of every mesh into a world-sized shadow window. For the walkable build on this class of
machine the split is the recommendation: keep the compile + texture half (14 s, the part that
removes the compiles) and replace the warm pass by the pool's own pre-fetch from the spawn
(§5.1 — with the caps raised the parts near the spawn are resident before the first frame
anyway), or scope the warm pass to the meshes within the pre-fetch radius of the spawn.

**Triangles.** Three systems are 80 % of every view: the trees (2.5–2.9 M, 30 %), the vegetation
(1.7–2.2 M, 21–27 %) and the structures (1.1–1.9 M, 14–23 %). Nothing else reaches 10 %. The
trees' share is the far tree — the whitebark and column families' LOD sets, the giants' sector
meshes and their far canopies — not the near LOD, which the fixed cameras never see (§5.3).

**Draw calls.** The **character group issues 129 draws for 0.17 M triangles** — Link's parts,
each Kokiri kid's parts, Navi and the shadow discs as separate meshes, each drawn again into the
shadow map; a quarter of the frame's calls for two per cent of its triangles (the round-9 INBOX
already put this at 120–150 recoverable calls). The vegetation (104–108) and the structures
(51–103) are the other two large call budgets; the trees' 56–66 are already one InstancedMesh per
variant and LOD.

**Milliseconds.** Standing still at A costs 154 ms a frame on this box against 79–100 ms at the
other five — A has the most draw calls (521), the most triangles (8.80 M) and, per the render-issue
column, the most CPU-side work in three's draw loop (43 ms of JavaScript before the GPU even
starts, against 18–31 elsewhere). Per §3 the GPU part is per-pixel: the views differ in what they
cover of the shadow map's window and the composer's screen-space passes, not in geometry (F, the
second-heaviest in triangles, is the fastest frame). The savings in §6 are therefore ranked by
what they take off the pixel pipeline and off three's issue loop, not off the triangle count.

## 5. Ablations — swap distances 18 / 25 m and pre-warming

Four scratch builds of `973a21e` (`gauntlet/perf/r48/build-variants.mjs`, exact diffs in
`variants.json`), each walked with the same trace as the baseline and captured at the six fixed
views. The lod variants neutralise the per-bole override bands (`NEAR_BASE_RADIUS_OVERRIDE`,
12–22 m, chosen as the widest each fixed camera allows; `seat-7` keeps its 5 / 7 m hero
protection) so that "every base swaps at N m" is what is measured, and raise `NEAR_CANOPY_MAX_Y`
with the canopy radius the way the shipped 21 m follows 22 m.

| variant | near base in / out | near canopy in / out (lobe cap) | pre-fetch canopy / base | pools canopy / base | build budget |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| baseline (as shipped) | 10 / 13 m (+ overrides) | 22 / 26 m (21 m) | 34 / 22 m | 64 / 12 MB | 3 ms |
| `prewarm` | as shipped | as shipped | as shipped | **192 / 32 MB** | **6 ms** |
| `lod18` | **18 / 21 m** | **26 / 30 m (25 m)** | 42 / 30 m | 64 / 12 MB | 3 ms |

### 5.1 Pre-warming alone: the pool cap is the whole hitch story

`prewarm` changes nothing the player sees — swap distances as shipped, every frame's draw calls
and triangles identical to the baseline's segment for segment — and only lets the pools hold what
the pre-fetch radius asks for:

| | baseline | `prewarm` |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| canopy pool live bytes | 64 MB (cap, from frame 29) | 186.5 MB (all 369 parts resident — the demand is under the cap, so nothing is ever dropped) |
| canopy builds / evictions / synchronous builds on the walk | 264 / 504 / 1 | **0 / 0 / 0** |
| base pool builds / evictions | 19 / 32 | 0 / 0 |
| build work on the walk (`workMsTotal`) | 10.8 s | 0.09 s |
| trees.update median / p95 | 3.9 / 23.0 ms | **0.6 / 2.8 ms** |
| world.update median | 16.6 ms | **10.0 ms** |
| vegetation.update median / p95 | 8.9 / 40.1 ms | 7.1 / 31.3 ms (the CPU it shared with the builds) |
| geometry uploads on the walk | 200 | 201 |

The pool as shipped is a third of its demand (§3), so every part the walker approaches is
rebuilt — the same deterministic geometry, 264 times — and evicted again. With the cap above the
demand the measurement build made at load simply stays, and the trees system costs 0.6 ms a
frame. **Cost: ~140 MB more resident geometry** (canopy 64 → 186 MB, bases 12 → 29 MB) — on the
owner's 62 GB laptop with an integrated GPU sharing system memory, nothing; the reason round 42
capped it was the 173 MB of round 41 on a 4-core CI VM. The right shape is therefore not "prewarm
around the player" as a new mechanism but a cap that follows the machine: `deviceMemory`-scaled
(192 MB on ≥ 8 GB, the shipped 64 MB under it), which is what §7 recommends first. The
milliseconds of this run carry the box's noise (`mixed` spikes, GPU finish 104 ms median against
100) — read the update column and the counters.

### 5.2 Swapping at 18 m: what it costs, and why it needs the pools

`lod18` moves every near base to 18 / 21 m and the near canopy to 26 / 30 m (lobes up to 25 m);
`lod18prewarm` is the same world with the pools raised. The walk, segment by segment (the
counters; `lod18`'s milliseconds are from a run a diagnostic overlapped and are repeated in §5.4):

| | baseline | `lod18` | `lod18prewarm` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| draw calls, median / max | 422 / 480 | 427 / 486 | 427 / 486 |
| triangles, median / max | 9.16 / 11.42 M | 9.23 / 11.57 M | 9.23 / 11.57 M |
| near-canopy parts (built for the world) | 369 | 413 | 413 |
| canopy pool live bytes | 64 MB (cap) | 64 MB (cap) | 192 MB cap, 374 of 413 resident |
| canopy builds / evictions / **synchronous builds** on the walk | 264 / 504 / 1 | 353 / 636 / **39** | 260 / 299 / **0** |
| canopy build ms p50 / p95 / max · longest chunk | 26.9 / 117 / 243 · 232 | 14.1 / 51 / 125 · 79 | 12.4 / 21.5 / 40.7 · **11** |
| base pool builds / evictions / synchronous | 19 / 32 / 0 | 22 / 35 / 3 | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| demand inside the pre-fetch radius, canopy / base | 125 / 11 MB | 185 / 19 MB | 185 / 19 MB |
| trees.update median / p95 | 3.9 / 23.0 ms | 3.7 / 10.0 | **0.5 / 7.2** |
| vegetation.update median / p95 | 8.9 / 40.1 ms | 6.9 / 37.5 | 5.4 / 11.4 |
| world.update median | 16.6 ms | 12.7 | **8.3** |
| step median / p95 / p99 | 142 / 243 / 360 ms | (overlapped) | **115 / 159 / 283** |
| frames over 2× the median | 63 | (overlapped) | **39** (37 of them `mixed` = the box) |
| geometry uploads on the walk | 200 | 210 | 202 |

What the 18 m swap costs the GPU is **+1–2 % triangles and +2–5 draw calls per segment** — on a
per-pixel-bound frame, nothing measurable. What it costs the CPU with the pools as shipped is
real: the demand inside the pre-fetch radius grows from 125 to 185 MB against a 64 MB cap, so the
walk needs **39 synchronous builds** (a part that must be drawn before its chunked build is done:
the frame waits for the whole build, up to 125 ms) and 636 evictions. With the caps raised the
same walk has no synchronous build, the chunked builder's longest step is 11 ms (the 6 ms budget
is honoured; at 3 ms the shipped builder ran single steps of 232 ms — the budget check sits
between yields that are too far apart for the round-46 parts), and the whole frame is the
smoothest of the four runs: p95 159 ms against the baseline's 243, and the 39 remaining spikes
are the box's, not the trees'. The pool still churns (260 builds, 299 evictions: 413 parts at
~0.5 MB do not all fit in 192 MB) without a hitch, because every build now finishes ahead of its
swap. **18 m swaps are affordable only together with the pool change; alone they make the
walk hitch more than the shipped 10 m.**

## 7. The brief for `lod-1`

What the owner asked for — "trees spawn high quality immediately around me" and "run more
efficiently" — comes apart into four changes in `src/world/trees/` (index.ts, giant.ts,
nearCanopy.ts, lodPool.ts) and one outside it, in this order:

1. **Size the LOD pools to the machine, not to the CI VM.** `NEAR_CANOPY_POOL_BYTES` /
   `NEAR_BASE_POOL_BYTES` 64 / 12 MB → 192 / 32 MB when `navigator.deviceMemory ≥ 8` (keep the
   shipped caps under it and under a headless capture, so CI and the fixed frames are untouched —
   the frames are byte-identical either way, §5.3). Measured: 264 builds / 504 evictions / the
   trees' 3.9 ms median update on the walk → 0 / 0 / 0.6 ms, world.update −6.6 ms (§5.1). This is
   the single largest runtime saving in the document and it costs 140 MB of resident geometry.
2. **Then raise the default swap to 18 / 21 m (bases) and 26 / 30 m (canopy, lobe cap 25 m), with
   the per-camera bands re-derived** — not with the override table dropped. The rule already in
   giant.ts (no fixed camera inside a band of a bole it frames) applied to 18 m means:
   `stair-bank-giant` stays at its shipped 10 / 13 (13.6 m from F, in F's right edge — it alone is
   F −0.0086 at 18 m), `plaza-south` ≤ 16 / 18 (17.7 m from F), `seat-7` at 5 / 7 as today; every
   other bole and every lobe takes the default. With that, all six frames hold (A, C, D
   byte-identical; B, E +0.0002 / +0.0003) and the walker on the plaza, the stairs and the north
   path meets detailed boles and layered lobes from 18 m instead of 10 (§5.3). Pre-fetch radii
   +8 m with the swap (30 / 42 m) so the 7.5 s lead at walking speed is kept.
3. **Honour the build budget.** With the shipped 3 ms budget the chunked builder ran single steps
   of 232 ms (29 ms even on a quiet box); at 6 ms with the raised caps the longest step on the walk
   was 11 ms (§5.2). The yield points in the near-canopy part builders (nearCanopy.ts, the
   twiglet / lamina sprays) are too far apart for the round-46 parts: yield per twig, not per lobe,
   and raise the budget to 6 ms on ≥ 8 GB machines. Together with (1) this makes the 18 m walk the
   smoothest measured run: p95 159 ms against 243 at the shipped settings, no synchronous build.
4. **25 m is the follow-up, not the first step.** It costs the frames the same one tree as 18 m
   (§5.4) plus C −0.0005 / D −0.0009 from 30 m canopy lobes at their edges, and it shows six near
   bases and 50 near-canopy parts around a standing walker — the demand inside its pre-fetch
   radius is the question the clean lod25 trace answers (§5.4); ship it only if the pool of (1)
   holds it without churn.
5. **What pays for it is not in the trees.** The GPU on this class of machine spends ~78–100 ms a
   frame on pixels regardless of geometry (§3); the ranked per-pixel savings are in §6 (shadow
   map size and taps, the composer stages, the render scale), and the largest draw-call lever in
   the scene is the character group's 129 calls for 0.17 M triangles (§4) — both outside the
   trees lane. Play mode already draws 9.2 M triangles at the median (11.4 M on the stairs), over
   the 9 M hero budget; the near-LOD change adds 1–2 % to that.

Acceptance for the lane, as the measurements define it: `perftrace.mjs --finish` on the walk with
0 synchronous builds and a longest build chunk ≤ 2× the budget (`poolSeries` in the trace), the
six fixed views within −0.003 of the take it branches from on **both** SwiftShader and the native
GPU (the native baseline of a commit is `capture.mjs` with `ZR_NATIVE_GPU=1`, §1), and the
`sn-bole-*` / `w22-stairs-u` / `w27-plateau-u` survey poses showing the near versions from 15–18 m
where today they show the far ones.
