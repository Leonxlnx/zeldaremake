# Performance profile of the sealed world — take-0116 (`973a21e`), native Radeon 780M

**fable-6, 2026-09-19.** The brief for fable-cursor's `lod-1` lane. The owner's two asks were
"trees should spawn high quality immediately around me" and "the game should run more
efficiently"; this document measures what the sealed world costs on the owner's own machine, what
moving the trees' near-LOD swap distances out to 18 / 25 m and pre-warming the LOD pools around
the player would cost, where the frame's triangles and draw calls go, and which savings would pay
for it — ranked.

Everything here is a **native** measurement: headless Chrome on the owner's laptop (AMD Ryzen 7 260
/ Radeon 780M, 16 threads, 62 GB, Windows 11) with `ZR_NATIVE_GPU=1` (ANGLE on D3D11), 1280×720,
`quality=high`, the fixed-timestep play mode of `gauntlet/scripts/perftrace.mjs`. The numbers are
comparable with Astra's and owner-fable's native runs and **not** with the monitor's SwiftShader
takes. The laptop is shared with astra-local and owner-fable, who capture on it too: every table
says what else was running (`gauntlet/perf/r48/load.log`); the counters (draw calls, triangles,
pool bytes, builds, evictions) do not depend on the load, the milliseconds do.

Evidence: `gauntlet/perf/r48/` (every trace JSON with per-frame rows, the ablation matrix, the
variant provenance, `load.log`).

## 2. The near-LOD machinery as shipped in `973a21e` (read-only facts)

The trees system (`src/world/trees/index.ts`) keeps two "near" LOD layers that only exist around
the live camera:

| layer | what it is | swap in / out | pool cap | pre-fetch radius | shown at once |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| near base (`giant.ts` NEAR_BASE_*) | the lower 5 m of every giant / seated column as a relief bole with buttress fins and foot plants; the plain sweep's lower rings collapse meanwhile | **10 / 13 m** (per-bole overrides 5–18 m in NEAR_BASE_RADIUS_OVERRIDE, chosen so no fixed camera stands inside a band) | 12 MB (≈ 9 of 22 bases) | 22 m | NEAR_BOLE_SLOTS = 6 |
| near canopy (`nearCanopy.ts` NEAR_CANOPY_*) | every eligible lower lobe (centre ≤ 21 m up) of a giant / column rebuilt as twiglets with layered laminae; the far cards / laminae of that lobe fold away in the colour pass only (shadows unchanged) | **22 / 26 m** (3-D to the lobe centre; a lobe a hero camera frames swaps at that camera's distance − 1.5 m, never under 7 m) | 64 MB (≈ 140 of 364 parts) | 34 m | NEAR_CANOPY_SLOTS = 40 lobes + 12 limb dressings |

Both layers live in an LRU geometry pool (`lodPool.ts`, round 42): a part inside its pre-fetch
radius is rebuilt ahead of its swap distance in chunks under a **3 ms per-frame budget**
(NEAR_LOD_BUILD_BUDGET_MS), evicted past the byte cap (least recently used first, then the farthest
wanted), and a part that must be drawn before its build is ready is finished synchronously in the
frame (`syncBuilds` in the report). The comment on the cap says 64 MB "pre-fetches to about the
26 m out-radius"; the report below says what that means on the walk.

Two design consequences matter for the brief:

1. **The fixed frames are protected by distance, not by a flag.** Every swap radius was chosen so
   that no hero camera stands inside a band of a bole / lobe it frames (giant.ts lists the
   distances: A's stair-bank giant 10.2 m off-frame, D's north-west-near 11.6 m at its edge, C's
   9.8 m behind it). Moving the default in-radius to 18 or 25 m puts several hero cameras inside
   the bands — the six-view captures per variant below measure exactly that.
2. **"Immediately high quality around me" is two different costs**: the swap distance (how far
   away a tree is already detailed — triangles per frame) and the pre-fetch / pool (whether the
   detail is *built* before the walker gets there — hitches). The variants separate them.

## 3. Baseline — the sealed world on the walk

`trace-0116-baseline.json` — 2400 fixed steps (40 s) along the plaza → stairs → house → north-path
walk, 1280×720, `--finish`. Box: no sibling captures, but the CPU at 99–100 % from the owner's other
apps throughout (Codex desktop, the Claude app's Chrome, Blender open), which is why the world
built in 254 s here against Astra's 52 s in round 37 — read the medians as an upper bound and the
*split* of the frame as the finding.

| | median | p95 | p99 | max |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **step (JS + GPU completion)** | **142.4 ms** | 242.6 | 360.2 | 5834 (frame 0: 11 programs compiled) |
| world.update (JS) | 16.6 | — | — | 293 |
| render issue (JS) | 22.5 | — | — | 4771 (frame 0) |
| **GPU finish (waiting for the frame)** | **99.8** | 267 | 298 | 425 |
| draw calls | 422 | 475 | 480 | 480 |
| triangles | 9.16 M | 11.1 M | 11.3 M | 11.42 M |

**What the split says.** About 100 of the 142 ms are the GPU finishing the frame, and that number
does not move with the geometry: standing still at the spawn (8.1 M triangles, 421 draws) the
finish is 98 ms; climbing the stairs (10.7 M, 450 draws) it is 100 ms; the correlation between a
frame's triangle count and its GPU wait over the whole walk is **0.04** (0.6 ms per extra million
triangles). The 780M is not vertex-bound on this world; it is **per-pixel bound** — the 4096² sun
shadow map with 12 PCSS taps on every lit fragment, SSAO at half resolution, the god-ray march,
bloom, the softening pass and the tone/grade composite at 1280×720 (the round-38 SwiftShader
matrix in `gauntlet/perf/ABLATIONS.md` pointed the same way: `scale=0.5` −33 %, `fx=off` −28 %,
`shadow=0` −15 %). §5 and §6 measure those knobs natively. The consequence for `lod-1`: **wider
near-LOD swap distances cost the GPU almost nothing on this machine; what they cost is on the CPU
side, in the pool** — which is where the walk's hitches already come from.

**Play mode is over the hero budget.** The fixed hero views draw 8.8 M triangles (W38's ceiling is
9 M); the follow camera at 1.75 m behind Link sees 9.2 M at the median and 11.4 M on the stairs,
because the vegetation's near LOD and the grass tiles ring the camera and the stairs look down over
the plaza's full carpet. Nothing in the rubric measures this; it is the frame the owner plays.

**The hitches.** 63 frames exceed 2× the median (285 ms). Their causes (`spikeCauses`):

| cause | frames | what it is |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `mixed` | 41 | update + issue + finish all elevated — the box's load (other processes) landing on a frame |
| `update:vegetation` | 7 | vegetation re-buckets (≥ 0.6 m of camera travel): **211 ms** at frame 87, 153 ms at 1719, 105 ms at 1839; 386 frames of the 2400 spend > 20 ms in vegetation.update (15.7 s of the 40 s walk) |
| `render-issue` | 6 | three's CPU side of the draw — 3.4 s at frame 276 (`stairs up`) with one program compiled |
| `gpu-upload` | 4 | first uploads: 200 geometries over the walk, most of them the trees' pool builds and grass tiles coming into range |
| `update:trees` | 3 | the near-LOD builds: 141 frames spend > 20 ms in trees.update (5.9 s of the walk), max **233 ms** in one frame |
| `shader-compile` | 2 | 12 programs compiled after the first frame (the depth/colour variants a pose meets first) |

**The LOD pools on the walk** (`poolSeries`, one report every 30 frames):

| | near canopy | near base |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| cap | 64 MB | 12 MB |
| live bytes, whole walk | 63.5–64.0 MB (pinned at the cap from frame 29) | 11.3–11.8 MB (at the cap) |
| parts / resident at the end | 369 / 129 | 22 / 9 |
| builds / evictions in 40 s | **264 / 504** | 19 / 32 |
| synchronous builds | 1 | 0 |
| build ms p50 / p95 / max | 26.9 / 117.1 / 243.3 | — |
| longest single chunk between yields | **231.7 ms** (budget 3 ms) | 0.3 ms |
| demand inside the pre-fetch radius | 125.5 MB at 34 m (48 MB at 22 m, 75 MB at 26 m) | 11.3 MB at 22 m |
| pinned (drawn) bytes | 19–23 MB | 0 |

Two things are wrong with the shipped tuning, and they are what "trees spawn high quality
immediately around me" runs into:

1. **The canopy pool is a third of its demand.** The parts within the 34 m pre-fetch radius weigh
   125 MB on this walk; the cap is 64 MB, so the pool evicts what it just built to make room for
   the next approaching part — 504 evictions and 264 builds in 40 s, 6.6 builds a second at 27 ms
   each in the median. That is 7 s of the 40 s walk spent rebuilding parts the walker had already
   passed, and the "drawn" set is only 19–23 MB. The comment in `index.ts` (64 MB "pre-fetches to
   about the 26 m out-radius") was true for the round-42 world; the round-46 canopy is bigger.
2. **The 3 ms build budget is not honoured.** The chunked build yields between its steps, but a
   single step ran 232 ms; the p95 of a whole build is 117 ms and the max 243 ms — a build that is
   meant to spread over 80 frames lands in one or two. These are the `update:trees` spikes and part
   of the `mixed` ones. The generator's yield points are too coarse for the round-46 near-canopy
   parts (twiglets + up to 2400 laminae each).

**Load time.** `ready` 254 s natively under this load (world build 134 s: terrain 37 s, structures
33 s, vegetation 25 s, trees 13.5 s, rocks 11 s). The `--warmup` run (`?warmup=1`, what the
walkable build does before its first frame) built in 359 s and spent **106 s in the warm-up**
(compile 17.9 s, textures 4.9 s, the warm pass — every mesh visible, the shadow window widened to
the world — **83 s**); it then lost its page ("Target closed") on the first traced frame — at the
minute another headless Chrome (the site's QA screenshots) was launched beside it, which is the
failure mode found later in the matrix: **two puppeteer launches within seconds of each other on
this laptop detach one of the pages** ("frame got detached" / "Navigating frame was detached"
before the world loads). Every capture in this document after that discovery ran alone; the
warm-up retry is in §5. The 106 s warm-up on an integrated GPU is a number for the walkable build
regardless: on this box it is 40 % of the time from click to first frame.
